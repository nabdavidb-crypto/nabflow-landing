Before you reach out

The questions owners actually ask

Will it sound like a robot?

Judge that by hearing it, not by reading about it. It holds a normal conversation, copes with being interrupted, and tells the caller up front that it is an AI assistant and that a person is available. If it doesn't sound right for a premium clinic, it doesn't belong on your line — and I would rather you say so before it goes live than after.

What happens when it can't answer?

It says so and passes the call to a person. It does not give medical advice, assess whether someone is a candidate for a treatment, discuss medications, contraindications, pregnancy or health history, or quote a firm price on anything sold per unit or by range. That list is decided before the script is written, not patched in afterwards.

Does it work with our booking system?

It is built to book into what you already use — Vagaro, Mangomint, Aesthetic Record, Fresha and similar. If yours doesn't connect cleanly, you will hear that on the fifteen-minute call rather than three weeks into a build.

How is patient information handled?

As information you are accountable for under PHIPA. Every call opens with an AI disclosure and a chance to decline before anything is collected. Calls are logged so there is a record of what was collected and when. There is a written data-handling agreement, and client information is not used for anything other than running your service. The agent collects what a booking needs and nothing clinical.

Will it replace our front desk?

No, and most owners don't want it to. It takes the calls nobody could get to and the questions the desk answers twenty times a day. Your team stays with the client in front of them.
